02

A prep sheet before every interview

When you land an interview, tell me in your portal. Before you walk in, you get a prep sheet for that company and that role, built from your own history, so your answers are real stories and not memorized lines.

What's in it

  • The role in plain words, and what they'll care about most
  • Company facts worth saying out loud
  • 10 to 15 likely questions, with answer outlines from your own experience
  • A practice scenario or two
  • Good questions to ask them
  • Who you're meeting, where, and what to bring

Practice on your phone

The likely questions also show up as flip cards in your portal. Read the question, answer out loud, flip to check your outline.

Prep sheet · Patient Access SupervisorThursday 10:00 AM · on site · 30 minutes

What they'll care about most

Keeping authorizations from stalling and the front desk calm on busy days. Expect questions about volume, denials and leading people.

"Tell me about a time a patient was upset at check-in."
  • Situation: Monday rush, a prior auth was missing for a scheduled MRI.
  • What you did: called the insurer while the patient waited, offered the next open slot.
  • Result: auth approved the same day, patient kept the appointment.
"How would you handle a team member who keeps making verification errors?"
  • Use your checklist story: you wrote it after repeated errors, and errors dropped.
  • Close with how you'd check in weekly, not wait for a review.

Ask them

"What's the biggest cause of authorization delays here right now?"

03

Help when the offer comes

An offer is the moment most people leave money or benefits on the table. Send me the offer and I'll go through it with you before you answer.

What I look at

  • Pay against the range they posted and typical pay for the job in your state
  • Benefits, paid time off, schedule, and remote or in-office days
  • Start date, commute, and anything in writing that doesn't match what you were told

What you get

  • A plain-English summary of the offer
  • A short counter email or call script, written in your words, if asking for more makes sense
  • A quick call to talk it through, if you want one
Offer review · Patient Access SupervisorExample

The numbers

Offer: $57,000. Their posted range: $55,000 to $68,000. Dana has 4 years doing exactly this work, so asking toward the middle of their own range is reasonable.

Counter email, first lines
  • "Thank you, I'm excited about this role. Given my four years running prior authorizations in Epic, I'd like to ask whether you can bring the base salary to $62,000."

The decision is always yours, and there's no guarantee an employer will move. Many have some room; some don't. Asking politely, with a reason, is a normal part of accepting a job.
