Guide · Resumes

Numbers make a resume believable.

"Responsible for payroll" tells an employer almost nothing. "Ran biweekly payroll for 1,200 employees" tells them a lot.

From the way we rewrite client resumes every week.

The rule we never break: every number has to be true and something you could explain in an interview. A rough honest number ("about 40 calls a day") beats a precise invented one.

Four kinds of numbers almost everyone has

  1. People: how many customers, patients, employees or coworkers you served, managed or trained.
  2. Volume: calls a day, orders a week, claims a month, invoices, appointments, tickets.
  3. Money: the size of the budget, sales, accounts or payroll you handled.
  4. Time and accuracy: how fast you turned things around, how often you hit a deadline, how few errors you made.

How to find your numbers

  • Think about a normal day or week, then multiply. Fifty orders a day is about 250 a week.
  • Check old performance reviews, sales reports, emails about targets, or your manager's thank-you notes.
  • When you're not sure, say "about" or use a range you'd stand behind.
  • Leave out anything confidential, like client names or exact revenue, if your employer would mind.

The formula

Action verb + what you did + a number + the result. For example: "Cut invoice errors by about half by adding a two-step review for every batch."
