Guide · Free help

Free job help most people never use.

New Jersey and New York both offer free career counselors, workshops and, for many people, money for job training. Here's what's available and how to get it.

Last checked October 17, 2026, against New Jersey Department of Labor and New York State Department of Labor pages. Programs and rules change, so confirm details with your local center.

Worth knowing: if you're between jobs or changing careers, your state may help pay for training that leads to a better-paying job. You have to ask.

New Jersey: One-Stop Career Centers

New Jersey runs One-Stop Career Centers (also called American Job Centers) across the state. The state says counselor services are free. Services vary a little by center, and typically include:

  • One-on-one career counseling
  • Jersey Job Club workshops on resumes, interviewing and job search
  • Resource rooms with computers and phones you can use for your search
  • English (ESL) and high school equivalency classes
  • Job training, apprenticeships and on-the-job training
  • Extra help for veterans, workers 55 and older, people with disabilities, young adults, and people returning from incarceration

Find your center: the state lists every location on its One-Stop Career Centers page. In Monmouth County, the center is at 60 Taylor Ave N, Neptune, NJ 07753, (732) 481-6333, open Monday to Friday, 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM. Walk-ins are welcome for most career services; some services, like help with an unemployment claim, are by appointment.

Money for job training in New Jersey

Through a federal program called WIOA, New Jersey can pay for job training for eligible adults and people who lost their jobs. The state's current guidance recommends up to $7,500 per training account, and local areas can set their own limits, including higher ones. A few rules apply:

  • The school or course must be on the state's approved training provider list.
  • The training has to lead to a job that's in demand.
  • If you qualify for a federal Pell Grant, that's used first.
  • Your center has to agree that you need training to find suitable work.

Eligibility is decided by your local One-Stop Career Center, so start there. Ask your center which programs near you are approved for the jobs you want.

Free online courses and tools in New Jersey

  • SkillUp NJ: free online courses and certifications, listed on the NJ Department of Labor career services page.
  • My Career NJ (mycareer.nj.gov): the state's career site, with tools to explore careers and search for training.
  • Job fairs and hiring events are listed on the NJ Department of Labor career services page, under Job Fairs and Targeted Recruitments.

New York: Career Centers and the Virtual Career Center

  • Career Centers: free career counseling, resume help and workshops across the state, plus referrals to job training for people who qualify. Find yours with the Career Center locator.
  • Virtual Career Center: an online tool from the NY Department of Labor with job matches, virtual career fairs, live webinars and mock interview practice. You sign in with an NY.GOV account. Learn more.
